# Sheepdog

A docked panel for [sheepdog](https://github.com/kccarlos/herdr-sheepdog), the daemon that lets you manage your coding agents by voice. Docked in the sidebar it is the whole panel: the voice's state and who holds it ("Voice on iPhone"), how many agents need you, voice controls, the question to answer, a composer to type to Sheepdog with the suggested replies to tap, and the conversation you also see on the phone. The header menu opens Settings, pairing and New conversation; an agent's name opens its details. A sidebar too narrow for that shows a compact rail, which opens the same panel as a window. (A `preview.png` is added with the screenshots; there is none yet.)

## What you see

**One block, two shapes.** Docked on the right (340 px by default, on the `chrome` background) it is the panel below. Narrower than 160 px it is the rail; a window (the rail opened it, or `mod.open` did) is always the panel. "Needs you" is the phone's amber, `#D98718` on a light sidebar and `#F39C1F` on a dark one (told from the `chrome` colour's luminance, since `theme()` has no light flag), used wherever this text says `blocked`; the theme's own `blocked` is red.

**The rail** is a compact column: the logo (26 px), the voice dot, and, when agents need you, a `blocked` badge with the count; the whole column is the button (it opens the panel as a window) and its tooltip says the state. The dot is `done` while listening, `idle` while muted, `working` while connecting, speaking or waiting for the voice, `blocked` in setup needed and `dim` otherwise.

The voice's words, from the daemon's `voice` in `/api/state`: "Voice off", "Connecting", "Voice not connected", "Waiting for the voice", "Ready" (nobody holds the voice), "Listening", "Muted", "Speaking" (this Mac holds it), and "Voice on iPhone" (or "…on a browser", else the client's name), with ", muted" or ", speaking" added, when another device holds it. This Mac is the holder whose `voice.client.platform` is `macos`.

**The panel**, the docked block or a 420 × 640 window, on the `chrome` background. Top to bottom: the header, the banners, the voice controls, the floating question, the composer, and the timeline filling the rest (6 below is a card opened over the timeline). The timeline always keeps at least 55% of the height. `model/layout.js` gives the header and composer their space first, then caps the banners, voice controls and question in a scroll area; a short panel makes each docking or setup banner one line and may make suggested replies one sideways-scrolling row. The composer is outside that area, so it stays visible.

1. A header, 12 px padding, a 1 px `border` under it, two lines. First: the logo (20 px), "Sheepdog" (13 px semibold), a `blocked` pill ("1 needs you", "2 need you") with `solid_foreground` text when agents need you, a **⋯** menu button, and, in a window, a ✕ that closes it. Second: the voice dot (7 px) and words (11.5 px, `muted`, truncated) and a **Technical** text button (11.5 px, `muted`). The controls stop the press so the rest of a window drags.
   - **Voice controls**, under the banners, in a 12 px padded bar with a 1 px `border` under it, when the daemon answers: four 26 px outline chips that wrap, **Talk**, **Mute**, **Stop** and **Hold to talk**; a chip that cannot work now is at 40% opacity and does nothing. Which work (model/voice.js's `voiceControls`): with nobody holding the voice only Talk (it runs `open sheepdog://talk`); with a holder (this Mac, or the phone, the daemon relays) Mute while not muted, Talk (unmute) while muted, Stop only while it speaks, Hold to talk always; with `voice.mode` "off" none, with "The voice is off." The Hold to talk chip sends `POST /api/voice/ptt {on:true}` on the mouse down and `{on:false}` on the mouse up (`solid` while held; a hold never released closes itself after 30 s, and closing the mod releases it). Talk unmutes with `POST /api/voice/mute {on:false}`, Mute is `{on:true}`, Stop is `POST /api/voice/stop`. When another device holds the voice, or a call answered 409, a **Use here** button (outline) runs `open sheepdog://talk` through `exec`. A 409 reads "No voice is connected" under the chips (a 503 gives the daemon's reason, no answer "Sheepdog did not answer. Try again."), and a failed `open` says "Sheepdog.app did not start. Install it with: brew install --cask sheepdog-app". Notes go after 8 s.

A `card` is markdown: headings in bold, bullet and numbered lists, fenced code in a `raised` monospace box, tables as a `raised` box with a bold header row and hairlines (`:---:` alignments kept), paragraphs; bold, italic and links are shown as their words. An `excerpt` is the agent's screen lines in monospace, an `approval` card its question, command and options read-only ("Answer it in the question card."), an `agent` card its title.

Every colour is a `theme()` name, so dark and light both work; the only computed colours are `blocked` and `working` and `done` with an alpha suffix. The light composer and assistant bubble switch between theme tokens for clearer separation without changing dark. The logo is `logo.svg` with its cream sticker outline, drawn with `image()` (which keeps colour; `svg()` would tint it to one).

## How it behaves

- The data layer asks the daemon: `GET /api/state` and `GET /api/conversation?limit=50` (and `&before=<next_before>` for older pages) with `fetch`, and `GET /events?kinds=card,conversation,daemon,pending,voice,web&since=<newest item id>` over a raw TCP socket (`net.connect`), because `fetch` is buffered. The overlays also read `/api/agents`, the voice and orchestrator model routes, `/api/settings`, `/api/devices`, `/api/pair` and `/api/pair/qr.json`; their changes use the matching `PUT`, the confirmed device `DELETE`, and the other routes described below. The mod reads the chunked HTTP body itself and parses `id:`, `event:` and `data:`. Every record, the `: keepalive` comments the daemon sends every 15 s included, proves the stream is alive; after 40 s without one it reconnects, waiting 1 s, then 2, 4, 8 and 15 s while that fails, and sends `Last-Event-ID`.
- An event that can change what is shown (a question, a push, an utterance, a reset, a restart, who holds the voice, mute, speaking) makes it ask `/api/state` and `/api/conversation` again, at most four times a second; transcript fragments wait for 1.5 s of quiet. Other events (audio stats, playback, usage) change nothing. Items merge by id, the server's copy winning.
- While the stream is down it asks every 3 seconds instead and shows "Live updates paused". If nothing answers the daemon is down, with the same 3 s retry.
- The list pages back when its last row is drawn: it asks for the next older page (`before`), and the rows are added below, so nothing moves under you.
- **Technical** shows every row the daemon sent; without it only what was said, what needs you, what agents did and where you are.
- A docked block 160 px wide or more is the panel (`.on_size_change` reports the width; before the first report it is taken as 340). Narrower, it is the rail. Clicking the rail opens one window with `key: "sheepdog-panel"`, `keep: true` and the prop `docked: true`: opening it again replaces it, and it returns after a restart. The ✕ closes it for good. `herdr-app do mod.open --name sheepdog` opens the same panel as a dialog without that key and without `docked`, so it shows the docking hint.
- **Docking.** A mod cannot put itself in the sidebar: herdr-app changes a layout only through `layout.set` (a whole tree, personal layouts only), and the tree's shape is not in the runtime's notes, so the mod does not guess it. herdr-app docks a mod the first time it sees the folder, into a layout you own, from `mod.json` (`place`, `size`). A shipped layout is replaced at start, so the window offers **Copy my layout** (`run("layout.clone", { name: shared("layout") })`); the user then adds a block for the mod `sheepdog` to the right side of the copy. That second step is the user's.
- **Voice.** The controls POST to the daemon, which relays them to whoever holds the voice (this Mac's Sheepdog.app or the phone), so the mod never asks who it is; the one thing it cannot relay is Use here, `exec("open", ["sheepdog://talk"])`. Hold to talk is a mouse down and a mouse up on one element (`on_mouse_down`, `on_mouse_up`); a release outside it is never seen, so a hold ends after 30 s on its own, and its on and off requests go in order.
- The header's **⋯** menu opens three separate overlays: **Settings** (voice and orchestrator models, languages, reply style, paired devices with a two-step Unpair, and `/api/state`'s update notice), **Pair a phone** (a QR drawn with `div()`s from `/api/pair/qr.json`, the `/api/pair` link as plain fallback text, and **New code**, which calls `/api/pair?new=1`), and **New conversation** (a confirmation before `POST /api/conversation/reset {via:"app"}`). AI-service consent is phone-local today and has no daemon route, so this desktop Settings has no consent toggle.
- A press on a timeline row that names an agent opens its details from `GET /api/agents`: what it works on, its last result and detail, its last question and answer, and its space. A card row still opens the card instead.
- The buttons **Run it in a tab** and **Run sheepdog setup** run `terminal.open` with `argv: ["sheepdog", "setup"]` in a tab.
- **Sending.** The textarea's `submit` event sends, never `change` (an input method's commit fires `change`). A textarea inserts the Enter's newline and also fires `submit`: with shift nothing is sent (the newline stays); without it the value minus ONE trailing newline is the text and the box is cleared; a value that is only whitespace never sends (and is cleared). `POST /api/turn {text}` carries an `Idempotency-Key` (`panel-<time>-<n>-<random>`). One line at a time: while one waits for its reply, Enter keeps the text.
- **Pending and reply.** The line shows at once as a dimmed bubble captioned "Sending…"; when `/api/turn` answers, the reply shows as a `dog` bubble ("Sheepdog wrote") and the panel asks `/api/conversation`. The daemon's own items are the record: the pending line is dropped when a typed `shepherd` item with the same words from the send on is in the conversation, the reply when a typed `dog` item after it has the reply's words (matched by id, each item claimed once), so nothing shows twice. One the conversation never shows goes after two minutes.
- **Failures.** 503 (the orchestrator is off) shows the daemon's reason under the box and puts the text back; 409 shows "Answered by voice." and keeps nothing (a spoken turn took the words); no answer at all shows "did not answer", keeps the text and the key, so sending the same text again replays the reply if the daemon did run the turn. After any status reply the next send gets a new key: the daemon keeps a refusal under its key too. A failed suggested reply leaves its button live.
- **Suggested replies** are the newest orchestrator reply's `options`, from its `voice.tool_result` event (the stream's one payload the panel reads; `voice` is already in `kinds`). Only the newest reply is live, and only until the user's words (said, typed, tapped or still pending), a newer reply (options or not), a new conversation, a daemon restart or the voice losing its context. A tap sends the option as a typed line. A reply that arrived before the panel was open, and is older than the newest conversation item, is not seen; it is not offered.
- It sends POSTs (`/api/turn`, `/api/pending/answer`, `/api/conversation/reset`, `/api/voice/mute`, `/api/voice/stop`, `/api/voice/ptt`) with an `Idempotency-Key`: no `/ws`, no keys, no files written. Nothing is written inside the mod folder at run time (herdr-app reloads a mod when its folder changes).

## Built from

- `main.js`: one `View`, `Sheepdog`. The docked block and a window are both the panel; `model/layout.js` says which shape by the width `.on_size_change` reports (the rail under 160 px, never in a window). Settings, pairing, agent details, New conversation and the header menu are separate overlay roles. Imports `View` from `gpui-kit`, `TextareaState` from `gpui-base`, `exists` from `fs/promises`, `connect` from `net`, and from `herdr`: `closeOverlay`, `exec`, `home`, `openOverlay`, `props`, `run`, `shared`, `theme`. It starts the data layer in a `cx.spawn` task and stops it in `close()` (which also releases the textarea and a held hold-to-talk); `init` makes the textarea, the outbox, the answers and the voice controller; `cx.timer.every(250, …)` pages back and drops what has stayed long enough; the size is also what the question's compact rule uses. The theme's `blocked` is replaced by `model/palette.js`'s amber.
- `mod.json`: `{ "place": "right", "size": 340, "title": "Sheepdog", "overlay": { "width": 420, "height": 640 } }`.

## Watch out

- **Newest first, with the composer above the timeline.** The owner chose it: the composer and the question sit at the top and the timeline is fully newest first, every item, no grouping by turn. Chat order (newest just above a composer at the bottom) was looked for and not found. `list()` ignores `track_scroll` (`herdr-app check` warns), so code cannot scroll it to the bottom; `v_virtual_list` takes a scroll handle (`scroll_to_bottom`) but wants each row's height, which wrapped text and cards do not have. The other candidate, a scroll area (`overflow_y_scroll` with an id) with `flex_col_reverse` and the rows reversed, builds every row (no lazy rows, so no "last row drawn" to page back on), starts at its top edge, and a reversed column's overflow lies above that edge, where a scroll offset does not reach; `herdr-app check` renders only the rail, so this cannot be shown to work without a person looking at the open window, and nothing here ships on a guess. If a later herdr-app lets `list()` anchor to its end (or a handle on it), switch `newestFirst` in `model/timeline.js` off and put the composer's pending line last (and the composer at the bottom).
- The rows of a `list()` may carry no handlers; a press on a row is the list's `on_item_click(key)` (the row id), which is how a card row opens. The question cards, the suggested replies and the composer are outside the list for the same reason. The phone's "N agent updates" group (it opens on a tap) is not drawn; every update is its own line.
- The textarea state (`TextareaState.new`) needs a live host call, so it is made in `init`, never in `render`, and released in `close()`. A mod folder write resets it (herdr-app reloads the mod), which is why nothing is ever written there.
- `herdr-app check` renders the docked block once at its default width, so the panel with no daemon; the rail, the rows with data, the window's docking hint, the overlays and the daemon states are only drawn at another width or when opened: look at them in herdr-app.
- `fetch` cannot stream (buffered, 8 MiB, 30 s) and the mod runtime has no `setTimeout`: the stream is `net.connect` plus `cx.sleep`, and a read that never returns cannot be given a timeout, so a watchdog task checks the silence every second. Closing the socket may not wake a pending read; the client races the read against the watchdog and `stop()`.
- No `TextDecoder` is assumed: `net/sse.js` decodes UTF-8 by hand. No `Intl` either: times are `HH:MM`.
- Each open copy of the mod has its own state, stream and poll: a docked block and the window its rail opened are two connections. A copy hidden in a column keeps its stream; it asks nothing while the daemon is quiet.
- The panel lists only what `GET /api/conversation` has (items with server ids) and the lines it is itself waiting on. The orchestrator's spoken replies and held pushes come over `/ws`, which this mod never opens; its suggested options are read from the `voice.tool_result` events on `/events`.
- A card's agent is focused with `run("agent.focus", { terminal: pane_id })`: with the herdr-core backend a card's `pane_id` is the agent's terminal address, which is what herdr-app calls its terminal. herdr-app says nothing back if no such agent is open; the mod shows a note only when `run` throws.
- Only plain `http://host[:port]` is read: the raw socket does not speak TLS.
- The block docks only into a layout you own; in a shipped layout use `mod.open` and **Copy my layout** (see Docking above). `layout.clone` and the layout block were read from herdr-app's command notes and not tried on every layout.
- Hold-to-talk needs the daemon's `/api/voice/ptt` and a holder that obeys it: Sheepdog.app first, the phone later. Until then a press is relayed and the phone ignores it.

## Done when

1. `herdr-app check herdr-app/mods/sheepdog` passes with no daemon running, and `make test-panel` passes.
2. With no daemon on the address the panel says "Sheepdog isn't running" with `sheepdog setup` and a **Run it in a tab** button (the rail's tooltip says it); starting the daemon turns it into the voice state within about 3 seconds, with no restart.
3. With a daemon the header's voice words match `/api/state` (`voice.mode`, `status`, `muted`, `speaking`, `client`): with the phone holding the voice it says "Voice on iPhone" and shows **Use here**.
4. A blocked agent adds an amber count to the header (and the rail) within about a second and a pointer to the timeline; answering it elsewhere turns the pointer into a settled line. The pointer's words stay on one line or wrap between the two parts, and it has normal space to the row below.
5. Dragging the sidebar under 160 px shows the rail and back over 160 px shows the panel; clicking the rail opens the panel as a window with no docking hint, while `herdr-app do mod.open --name sheepdog` opens one with it.
6. Mute, Stop and Hold to talk change the voice of whoever holds it (the phone's too), Stop is on only while it speaks, holding the button opens the mic and releasing it closes it; with nobody holding the voice a 409 reads "No voice is connected" with **Use here**; **Use here** starts Sheepdog.app, which takes the voice from the phone; with no Sheepdog.app in either Applications folder the buttons are off with `brew install --cask sheepdog-app`.
7. The panel's timeline matches the phone's for the same daemon: the same categories, settled questions as one line, the reconnect rule, restarts as dividers.
8. Reaching the bottom of the list loads the next older page until "This is the start of the conversation".
