# Speaker echo — 2026-10-03

Question (tier-2 finding 3, `docs/bench/tier2-2026-10-03.md`): with the
bot's own audio leaking back into the microphone, Gemini Live's
activity detection cuts the voice off on its echo dozens of times a run,
transcribes it as the CEO and answers itself. #108's text echo guard
catches the transcripts but cannot stop the cut-offs. What do the real
clients already do, how much echo is realistic, and what should the
daemon do about the rest?

## What the clients already do

- **iOS app** (`Sources/Sheepdog/AudioEngine.swift`): `AVAudioEngine`
  with `inputNode.setVoiceProcessingEnabled(true)` (the Voice-Processing
  I/O unit: echo cancellation, noise suppression, AGC); it turns bypass
  back off if something set it, and its diagnostics record whether the
  session is `.playAndRecord` / `.voiceChat`. The
  playback node sits on the same engine, so the canceller has its
  reference. The capture gate (`VoiceActivityDetector`, adaptive floor,
  6–10 dB open margin) runs on the **cancelled** signal, and `speaking`
  follows the gate.
- **Web `/voice`**: `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation` (the browser's
  AEC); the page reports `aec` in `ping` and tells the user to use
  headphones when the browser says it is off.
- Both send `playback` started / drained / flushed reports, so the
  daemon knows when the reply is audible.

## How much echo is realistic

- 3GPP TS 26.131 (terminal acoustics) asks for a weighted terminal
  coupling loss **TCLw ≥ 40 dB for hands-free** (≥ 46 dB at nominal
  volume, 55 dB recommended) and ≥ 46 dB for a handset. TCLw is measured
  at the send output, after the echo canceller: the residual a compliant
  phone may send is about 40–55 dB below what it plays. No published
  ERLE figure for Apple's VPIO turned up; the spec floor is the
  defensible number.
- The bench leaked the played audio back at **−15 to −18 dB**: a
  speaker next to a microphone with weak or no cancellation — the
  telephony case where Gemini Live is known to interrupt itself on
  reflected audio (Google AI forum, "acoustic echo cancellation needed
  for SIP telephony deployments") — not an iPhone with VPIO.
- Measured on the bench's audio (2026-10-03): the bot plays at about
  −20 dBFS (100 ms RMS while speaking, peak ≈ 26 000 LSB), the CEO's TTS
  lines about −20 dBFS. At −40 dB the echo peaks near 260 LSB, under the
  emulated iOS gate's 300 LSB detector floor, so the phone's gate never
  opens on it and nothing reaches the daemon; at −16 dB it peaks near
  4 000 LSB, opens the gate and streams to the model for the whole reply.

So the tier-2 failure models a phone **without** working echo
cancellation. With the app's VPIO working, residual echo should mostly
stay below the capture gate. The daemon still needs a safety net for the
cases where it does not: a speakerphone at full volume, VPIO bypassed
or degraded, a browser without AEC, a Bluetooth speaker.

## Options

| | What | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Half-duplex in the daemon: while the client reports playback, withhold mic audio unless the CEO is talking | Chosen, with a twist: the client's `speaking` cannot tell the CEO from the echo (the same detector opens on both), so the daemon tells them apart itself (below) |
| (b) | Client sends mic only while its detector says speech (hold-to-talk-like) | Already the case: the iOS gate only sends while open. It does not help when the echo opens the gate |
| (c) | Gemini Live config: `startOfSpeechSensitivity: LOW`, `prefixPaddingMs`, `activityHandling: NO_INTERRUPTION` | Session-wide, not "during playback": LOW / padding also make a quiet CEO harder to hear and only filter short echo bursts; NO_INTERRUPTION removes barge-in. Manual activity detection (`activityStart/End` from the daemon) needs the same CEO-vs-echo decision as (a) |
| (d) | Model the bench echo as post-AEC residual (−35 to −45 dB) plus a no-AEC worst case | Done: the bench runs below use −40 dB and −16 dB |

## The echo gate (`internal/voice/echogate.go`)

Automatic, no config knob, keyed on the playback reports; wired into the
Gemini session (`SendAudio`, `AudioStreamEnd`, the reader's audio,
transcripts and interruptions, playback reports, client attach). The
GPT-Live session is not gated.

**Unarmed until the client's echo shows itself.** A client whose echo
canceller works never sends the reply's echo, and must not pay for the
gate. So the gate starts unarmed — every frame goes out at once, as
today — and only watches. It arms, for the rest of that connection,
on the first of:

- two model self-interruptions that follow a microphone opening within
  400 ms of a reply's start (the speaker's echo opening the client's
  gate, the model cutting itself off within half a second — how a
  leaking phone shows itself first, too fast for an audio comparison);
- the reply's own words heard while it plays or within 3 s (input
  transcript sharing at least two word pairs, and half of its pairs,
  with the reply's output transcript);
- an audio match (below) at r ≥ 0.9 that holds its delay over 15
  successive windows (600 ms).

**Armed, it holds and compares.** While the client plays the reply
(started → drained, plus an 800 ms echo tail) each microphone frame is
held. The daemon knows what was played: the reply's audio as 10 ms block
powers on the play timeline, anchored on each `started` report (so the
~4× faster-than-real-time delivery does not matter). Each frame, the
last 320 ms of microphone blocks are compared with the current reply at
delays of −200 ms to 1 s (the client reports `started` a little after
its player begins; on the bench the echo sat 10–50 ms after the report).

- **Echo**: at some delay the log-energy envelopes correlate (r ≥ 0.7),
  less than 35 % of the window's energy rises 6 dB above the echo's
  level (the median microphone/reply ratio), and that level is no more
  than 6 dB above the level learned so far. Echo is dropped; the level
  and delay are averaged from it (the delay wanders as the client's
  player underruns).
- **Shepherd**: sound the reply does not explain, judged only once the
  window, shifted by the echo's delay, lies inside the current reply.
  The held frames (up to 960 ms) are released at once and the gate stays
  open until the client's silence gate closes (or ~1 s of echo alone).
- **Unsure** for 640 ms: let through. A missed echo costs one
  self-interruption (today's behaviour), a missed barge-in a CEO who
  cannot stop the voice.
- Frames that arrive in a burst (the client's pre-roll) are placed back
  in time and not judged until the burst is over.
- `voice.echo_gate` logs arming and each opening; `web.client.stats`
  carries `echo_withheld_frames`, `echo_released_frames`, `echo_opens`,
  `echo_armed`.

Unit tests (`echogate_test.go`, synthetic syllable-shaped speech with
10 ms flutter): echo at −10 … −30 dB and 40–200 ms delay stays out once
armed; arming takes ≤ 30 frames of the first reply; the shepherd's
speech over 30 replies never arms it; 45 barge-ins per condition (no
echo, −16 dB, −40 dB; starting with the reply, early, late) are never
lost, none late; an unarmed gate sends every frame exactly once; a
pre-roll burst stays out; a short "stop" before the client's gate
closes gets through.

The tier-2 campaign measured the same echo scenarios on main at
−15/−18 dB: 0/6 and 0/2, 171 of 258 daemon flushes, $0.24–1.20 a run.

What the numbers say:

- **−40 dB (a phone whose AEC works) is not a problem today.** On main
  echo-instruct passed 3/3 with no self-interruption: the residual never
  opens the client's capture gate. The gate stays unarmed there and
  changes nothing.
- **−16 dB (no working AEC):** main loses the reply to its own echo
  ~8 times a run and hears its own words (CEO-line WER 0.79). The shipped
  gate pays about two self-interruptions to arm — the first replies of a
  leaking session — and then holds the echo back: 2.2 self-interruptions
  a run, WER 0.20, a run costing ≈ $0.03 like a normal one. An
  always-armed gate does better on echo (0–0.7 a run) but holds every
  client back: barge-in without echo went from 617 to 844 ms (median),
  and on synthetic speech one barge-in in ten without echo came late or
  not at all. Arming on evidence keeps clients without echo exactly where
  main is (606 ms, nothing withheld, never armed in 12 runs).
- The normal scenarios ran on an always-on variant (no regression: 619
  vs 617 ms, 7/15 vs 8/15), not on the shipped gate: without audible
  echo the shipped gate never armed (12 runs) and an unarmed gate sends
  every frame once, as main does (unit test), so they were not re-run.
- **Barge-in is kept**: with echo every CEO line that began over a reply
  interrupted it (shipped gate 2 of 2, always-armed 3 of 3 at −16 dB;
  median 0.6–0.7 s, as on main without echo); without echo nothing
  changes.
- **Stricter audio criteria were tried and dropped**: requiring the echo
  to hold one delay and one level (ratio spread) rejected real echo,
  because the bench player's underruns move the echo's delay by hundreds
  of milliseconds within a reply; 17 false openings at −16 dB.
- **Pass rates are dominated by other failures**, the same on main:
  "Stop, stop. Is anything waiting for me?" is taken as the stop tool and
  answered "Nothing was in flight" without the question (echo-barge-in,
  every arm); the voice says "On it." before the push confirms
  (instruct rubric "confirm once"); brain answers like "Nothing yet" for
  a finished worker. These belong to the supervisor and brain, not the
  echo path.

## Limits and next

- The bench's echo is the played audio delayed and attenuated; a real
  room adds reverberation and a phone's speaker distorts. The arming
  rules and thresholds were tuned on it and on synthetic speech; tier 3
  (a real phone on speaker) should check them.
- Clients that time-stretch playback (the iOS app, 0.85–1.05) drift the
  echo's delay further; the gate follows a wandering delay once armed,
  but a drift beyond 1 s per reply is not covered.
- GPT-Live sessions are not gated.
- Once armed, the gate stays active 800 ms past the end of a reply (the
  echo tail), so a CEO answering right after a reply is held about
  250–300 ms before the first verdict releases the words. Only clients
  that leak echo pay it.
- The app could report its own measure (VPIO on, a residual estimate)
  so the daemon arms at once for a client known to leak; not needed for
  the bench.

Spend: ≈ $6.5 at list-price estimates over 131 tier-2 runs (the
always-on and stricter variants included), within the $10 budget.
