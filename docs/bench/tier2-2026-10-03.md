# Voice bench, tier 2: first campaign (2026-10-03)

The first end-to-end voice campaign: synthetic CEO speech (Gemini TTS on
Vertex) from a phone emulator that behaves like the iOS app, into scratch
daemons with the real voice model (Gemini Live `gemini-3.8-live` on Vertex)
and the real brain (DeepSeek `deepseek-flash`, failover to Vertex), over
the eval sim's scripted worlds. How it works: [README.md](README.md). Raw
reports (no audio): [results/2026-10-03/](results/2026-10-03/).

## Headline

| | Runs | Pass rate |
|---|---|---|
| All 34 scenarios × 3 | 102 (59 pass, 42 fail, 1 errored) | **58 %** |
| Without the two echo scenarios | 96 | 62 % |
| The 28 scenarios built on a tier-1 world | 84 | **57 %** — the same worlds score **89 %** in tier 1 (eval run C, DeepSeek) |

The voice layer costs about thirty points of pass rate on the same
conversations. Speech recognition itself is not the main reason (mean WER
of what the voice model heard of the CEO's lines: 7.6 % outside the echo
scenarios, 136 of 196 lines exact, zero lines unheard; café at 10 dB and
pink noise at 5 dB SNR made no difference). What fails is what happens
around the words: the voice model answering on the brain's behalf, a
deferral or a hesitation turning into a decline, the bot's own voice
leaking back into the microphone, cut and resumed pushes, markers read
aloud.

Spend: **≈ $13.5** in all, well inside the $40 budget (estimates at list
prices; the Live price is an unverified high-side guess, $3/M prompt and
$12/M response tokens): calibration $6.05 (one 19-minute Live session —
its context grows with every turn, which is why it cost more than the
whole campaign), campaign $5.30, the nine re-run barge-in scenarios
$1.24, smoke tests and an aborted first start ≈ $0.6, TTS ≈ $0.1. Per
run: median ≈ $0.04; the echo scenarios $0.24–1.20 (the voice model
talking to itself).

What calibration showed:

- **Gemini Live sometimes "transcribes" Mandarin as an English
  paraphrase** late in a long session: "先不要，等一下再说。" came back as
  "Not yet. Tell me later." and, three times, as "No, wait. Stop. Don't do
  that." — the English line spoken earlier in the same session. The
  campaign's fresh short sessions did not show it (Mandarin CER 8.8 %
  there), but the CEO's real sessions are long.
- A tired or hesitant delivery with pauses near a second splits one
  sentence into two turns at the app's 400 ms gate hangover; only the
  last clause reached the brain ("Let me know when they pass." for "Tell
  bench-claude to run the tests and let me know when they pass.").
- The emulator itself caught a contract trap: a binary frame sent after
  `silence on` cancels the daemon's comfort tail and `audioStreamEnd`,
  and every utterance is then transcribed only when the next one starts.
  Fixed in the emulator; worth checking that the iOS VoiceSocket never
  sends a frame after its `silence on`.

## Top findings

Ordered by what they cost the CEO. Each has the run, the evidence from
the daemon's events, and where to look.

### 1. The voice model answers for the brain, and the request is lost (P0)

The voice model says "On it." / "I'll check on the agents for you right
now." / "I don't have an update on bench-claude's specific task at the
moment." **without calling `to_supervisor`**. The daemon's relay watch
then counts that speech as an answer, so nothing reaches the brain and
nothing follows. An approval was lost this way:

```text
approve-claude-permission r3
  49.3s voice.transcript.in {"text":"Yes, make the edit."}
  49.3s voice.transcript.out "On " "it."
  49.9s voice.turn {"responded":true,"tool_calls":0}        -- no keys ever sent; the CEO waits 30 s in silence
status-en r1
  18.1s voice.transcript.in {"text":"What's going on?"}
  18.1s voice.transcript.out "I'll check on the agents " "for you right now."
  20.2s voice.turn {"responded":true,"tool_calls":0}        -- nothing more for the rest of the call
```

Outside the echo scenarios, 7 substantive CEO lines (an approval, status
questions, "Wait, just the important part.") went unrelayed this way
across 96 runs; the voice model's self-made answers are also where the
judge found invented facts. Leads: `internal/supervisor/inputs.go:205-216`
(`voiceSpoke` marks the input answered on any speech) and
`internal/supervisor/relay.go:22-27`; an acknowledgement that promises
work ("on it", "checking", "I'll check") with no tool call should not
count as an answer — relay it after the 4 s watch as if nothing was said.

### 2. A deferral or a hesitation becomes a decline (`esc`) (P0)

Five runs sent `esc` to bench-codex's open prompt although the CEO only
put it off. Codex's `esc` cancels the command:

| Run | The CEO said | Voice model heard | Brain |
|---|---|---|---|
| deferral-zh r3 | 先不要。 | 先不要。 | `send_keys esc`, pending.confirmed answer "no" |
| status-zh-tired r3 | 先不要跑，文档那边核对好了吗？ | 先不要跑。文档那边核对好了吗？ | `esc` |
| unheard-approval-requeue r2, r3 | No, don't run that yet. … | (same) | `esc` |
| deferral-en-hesitant r2 | "Uhh what does bench codex want" (TTS, checked by an independent transcription) | **"No.** What does bench Codex want?" | `esc`, then "Told it no, so nothing was pushed." |

The rule is in the prompt (`prompts/svp/RULES.md:9`: "not now", "later",
"先不要" put it off; decline only on a no), and tier 1 passed the H5
scenarios after #95 — over voice it still fails. The last row is the
dangerous one: a filler transcribed as "No." made an irreversible
decision on a line that was a question. Leads: the rail
(`internal/supervisor`, `send_keys` path) could refuse a decline when the
shepherd's input also asks a question or carries a deferral marker
("yet", "later", "not now", "先不要", "等一下"), and ask instead.

### 3. Speaker echo makes the voice model talk to itself (P0 for speakerphone use)

With the bot's own audio leaking back at −15 to −18 dB, 120 ms (a phone
on speaker whose echo canceller leaves a residue), all six echo runs
failed. The voice model transcribes its own speech as the CEO's, cuts
itself off, answers itself, and loops — in one run it drifted into
Spanish ("¿Quién va?" … "¡Hola! ¿En qué…" fifteen times):

```text
echo-instruct r1 (the CEO's line, as the voice model heard it):
  "Tell Bench Claude to run the tests and let me know when it's done.Sent it.I'mwaitingI'll let youTheeveryeveryEverything…"
  bot: "I'm standing by — (cut off)" "...until — (cut off)" "...everything — (cut off)" ×10
```

171 of the campaign's 258 daemon flushes came from the six echo runs,
which also cost 10–30× a normal run, and one acted on contaminated text
(echo-barge-in r3: a prompt to delete the two dead links nobody asked
to touch).
The iOS app depends entirely on the platform echo canceller. Leads: the
daemon knows from the playback reports when the client is playing
(`internal/voice/playtrack.go`); input that arrives only while the bot
plays, with the client's own detector closed (`speaking` off), could be
withheld or attenuated before it reaches Live (`internal/web/ws.go`
audio forwarding), and the app could report `aec` per session as it
already can in `ping`.

### 4. The voice model reads the push marker aloud (P1)

25 bot utterances in 14 of 102 runs began with the literal words
"**[system event, not spoken by the user]** bench-codex wants your
go-ahead…". The CEO hears the marker. Leads:
`internal/voice/gemini/tools.go:133` (the prefix) and
`prompts/assistant.md:3` (the instruction to read the message aloud);
also seen: a "<no speech>{pause}" placeholder spoken as text
(barge-in-hold-to-talk r1, the re-run).

### 5. A push the CEO talks over comes back as a fragment, over the CEO again (P1)

The heard-detection (#89) works — the cut push is marked unheard and
re-queued — but the re-queued push is sent while the CEO is still
talking, is cut again, and what the CEO finally hears is the resumed
tail:

```text
collision-finish-push r1
  30.3s web.client.flush; voice.push_unheard {"heard":"Bench-claude is done. It added the changelog entry you asked for, fixing", "reason":"user_turn"}
  33.0s voice.push_requeued; voice.push {"text":"Bench Clad is done. It added the changelog entry …"}
  33.4s voice.transcript.in {"text":"Also, did it touch the change log?"}
  34.0s played: "ignoring its first argument."            -- the CEO hears a fragment
  37.0s voice.push_unheard {"heard":"ignoring its first argument."}
```

collision-finish-push failed 3/3 (tier 1: 5/5). Note also "Bench Clad":
the brain adopted the misheard name from the transcript into its own
push. Leads: the re-queue timing in `internal/voice/gemini` (the
unheard/requeue path; it should wait for the shepherd's detector to
close plus the 300 ms gap), and re-sending a cut push whole rather than
letting the model resume mid-sentence.

### 6. A misheard name derails the promise and the finish is never told (P1)

"Let me know when bench-claude is done." was heard as "Oh, I know when
Bench Squad/Quad is done." (the TTS voice says "bench clot"; a real
speaker says it more clearly, so this is partly a TTS artefact). The
brain then claimed "Bench Squad is the agent you just renamed" (no rename
happened) and asked whether to report the finish instead of making the
promise; when bench-claude finished, the announce turn was skipped by the
chattiness ceiling (`brain.push_decision {"via":"ceiling","urgency_applied":"skip"}`,
`supervisor.push_quiet`, `internal/supervisor/announce.go:127-133`).
reconnect-held-push failed 3/3, collision-finish-push misheard the same
way. Lead: the brain should resolve a near-miss name to the roster
("Bench Squad" → bench-claude) or ask, never invent a rename; a "let me
know when" that names an unknown agent could still register the promise
for the closest match after confirming.

### 7. Failover is announced in provider jargon (P2)

With DeepSeek answering 503, every step failed over to Vertex (6
`brain.failover` events, the CEO got answers), but the daemon said
"**DeepSeek is failing; using Vertex for now.**" and the simulated CEO
then asked "What about the Vertex worker?" (`internal/brain/failover.go:296`).
The judge's right_agent / no_hallucination "no" in that run are
downstream of this line, not separate defects. Say "My thinking is slow
right now; answers may take a moment" or nothing.

### 8. A slow brain (+9 s per request) times the turn out (expected, P2)

slowness-brain-9s failed 3/3: a 3-step turn exceeds the 25 s utterance
timeout (`brain.fallback … context deadline exceeded`) and the CEO hears
the fallback or nothing until the next turn. The voice did hold the line
("Checking."). Gemini Live's own slowness could not be injected here (TLS
WebSocket to Vertex); the brain proxy delay stands in for "Vertex
slowness".

### 9. Smaller ones

- Options menus are read out verbatim ("Options: Yes, proceed; Yes, and
  don't ask again for this command; No, tell it what to do differently.")
  — the judge's most common ear_friendly "no".
- The brain sometimes answers with a fragment such as "2.0?" (the Apache
  2.0 option, approve-claude-askuser r2) and asks "Did you mean MIT when
  you said Mace?" about something the CEO never said.
- 8 bot utterances had audio but no output transcript; the CEO heard
  something the daemon cannot record as said.

## How far to trust these numbers

- **Judge noise.** Several "no" verdicts are downstream of one voice
  defect (the failover line, a cut push) or plain misreadings (in
  reconnect-held-push r2 the judge took the scripted worker's own
  changelog line for an action sheepdog sent). The findings above are
  read from the daemon's events, not from the verdicts.
- **Simulated CEO drift.** In deep-ask-detail the LLM CEO accepted
  sheepdog's offer to have the worker print the README, which the
  scenario's `no_actions` then counts as unintended. The behaviour is
  reasonable; the scenario's expectation is too strict for a free
  conversation.
- **TTS names.** The voices say "bench-claude" closer to "bench clot";
  part of the name mishearing is the synthetic speaker.
- **Attribution.** What the voice model "heard" of a CEO line is its
  input transcript between that line and the next; when lines are close
  (collisions, barge-in) a transcript can land in the neighbour's window,
  and in the echo runs the window holds the bot's own words (echo WER
  1.6).
- **Harness fix mid-campaign.** The barge-in habit was armed during the
  connect announcements too; the nine barge-in runs (barge-in-long-answer,
  barge-in-hold-to-talk, echo-barge-in) were re-run after the fix and the
  numbers above use the re-runs (before: barge_in 0/6). An earlier start
  was aborted after eight runs for a hand-out race in the client (fixed,
  PR #104).
- **The detector is idealised** (it hears the CEO's speech and the echo,
  not the room), and the player has no time-stretch; a real phone's
  detector misfires are tier 3's to find.
- **One run errored**: failover-brain-503 r3 hit a Vertex rate limit on
  the failover provider itself (counted as error, not in the pass rate).
