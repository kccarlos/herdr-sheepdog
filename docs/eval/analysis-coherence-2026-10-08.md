# Why does "coherence" fail? Analysis of the chatty baseline, 2026-10-08

Branch: `analysis/coherence-2026-10-08` (analysis only: no product code, prompt or test changed; no live run was needed, $0 spent).

Evidence: `docs/bench/results/2026-10-07-chatty/{a0,r}` (tier 1 workday ×5 = 35 runs per arm, DeepSeek `deepseek-flash` orchestrator and simulated user, `chatty`, Sonnet segment judge): `report.json` (transcripts, segment verdicts with quoted evidence), `events/*.jsonl`, `northstar.md`. Cross-check: `docs/bench/results/2026-10-05-workday-rejudged/t1`. Read first: `docs/glossary.md`, `docs/eval/README.md`, `docs/bench/README.md`, `docs/design/dual-brain.md`.

## Summary

1. **a0 and r are tier 1.** There is no voice model, no voice connection and no resume in this evidence. The coherence criterion's sentence "this comes after the voice connection was recycled mid-call (in a voice run)" is template text (`internal/eval/workday.go:181`) that does not apply here. The classes "the voice answered from its own short memory" and "resume recap missing it" are therefore structurally zero in these runs. Zero of the 3 compactions in 70 runs touched a failing run (`brain.compaction`: a0 long-haul r5 @139.8 s; r long-haul r5 @142.1 s; r zh-evening r1 @128.6 s, all after their probes and all passing). Orchestrator window use at the failing probes was 7.9k–11.4k tokens of the 12k budget, and only tool results are ever pruned (`internal/brain/engine.go`), so the user's early requests were still in the window.
2. **Most failures are not product failures.** Of the 17 failing coherence verdicts (a0 10, r 7), **13 are harness artefacts**: the probe is not in the segment that carries the `coherence_*` item (10 asked in another segment and answered correctly, 3 never asked). **4** are wrong or hedged answers, all in a0, none in r (one of the four, a0 explain-deep r5, was also mis-scored by drift but is a real wrong answer).
3. **The reverse also happens.** 9 of the 83 passes are credited with no probe in the scored segment. The same situation (probe answered elsewhere, correctly) got `yes` 5 times and `no` 10 times: a judge inconsistency, not a product one.
4. **The real product failures have one clean discriminator.** "First thing today" probes pass 19 of 20 in long-haul (second probe) and zh-evening (first-thing probe), both arms (the one miss is drift, r long-haul r2), where the first thing is a push the orchestrator itself said, and fail in explain-deep (a0 r1, r5), where the first thing is the user's own request. The orchestrator reaches for `recall`, and `recall` cannot return the user's own words **in tier 1**: it skips `KindRequest` (`internal/brain/tools.go:723`), and a typed tier-1 line is recorded only as a `KindRequest`. With a voice, the user's words are also recorded as `KindCEOUtterance` (`brain.go:641`, from the input transcription), which recall does search, so this part is probably a tier-1 / typed-input fidelity gap, not a voice-path defect (not verified on the voice path). `recall` also ranks newest-first, caps at 8 lines, and tokenises only ASCII (`queryWords`), which affects both paths.

## 1. The measure

`coherence_*` items are attached to a segment by the agenda beat number (`SegmentCriteria`, `internal/eval/segment.go:358-363`). The beat number on each user line is assigned by the harness, not by what the line said: any line the simulated user does not mark `[ANSWER]` or `[AGAIN]` is tagged "carries out beat `next`" (`agendaTurn`, `internal/eval/simuser.go:~240-250`). When DeepSeek answers a clarifying question without the marker (`notes-claude.`), says a silence placeholder (`[waiting]`) as text, merges two agenda items into one line, or repeats an earlier item, every later beat shifts and the `coherence_*` item lands on a line that is not the probe.

100 coherence verdicts (50 per arm; a0 = 40 yes / 10 no, r = 43 yes / 7 no):

| Class | a0 | r | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| yes, probe in the scored segment, answer right | 37 | 37 | a genuine pass |
| yes, no probe in the scored segment (vacuous) | 3 | 6 | credited without a test (a0 adversarial-afternoon r3, long-haul r3 seg11, morning-standup r5 seg13; r adversarial-afternoon r3, explain-deep r1, morning-standup r5 seg11 and seg13, zh-evening r3 seg11 and seg13) |
| no, probe asked elsewhere, answer right | 4 | 6 | beat drift (the rows below) |
| no, probe never asked | 2 | 1 | the run reached no probe |
| no, wrong or hedged answer (real) | 4 | 0 | see section 2 |

(The a0 explain-deep r5 row appears under "real" even though the judge's quoted reason was the drift; its real answer is wrong.) So the reported 80 % (a0) and 86 % (r) mix 9 untested passes with 13 untested fails (plus one real failure the drift hid). The honest statement is: among probes that were actually asked and scored in the right place, nearly all pass; the few real failures concentrate in one mechanism (section 2).

## 3. Contrast: passes

- **First-thing probes where the first thing is a push** (long-haul second probe and zh-evening first-thing probe: 19 of 20 across both arms pass; the miss is drift): a0 long-haul r1 seg17 (answered from a `recall` of 2 or from the window; "the first thing today was pricing-claude's fifty-dollar question"), r zh-evening r4 seg11 ("the first thing I told you was db-codex waiting on your go-ahead"). The first thing is an `assistant_said`/`push` line, which `recall` does return.
- **Short runs, answered from the window with no tool** (adversarial-afternoon r1/r2/r4/r5 both arms; morning-standup beat 13 "what did we start with", 9/10 passes): turns 1.3–3 s, no tool, window 6–9k tokens. Example a0 adversarial-afternoon r2 seg11 @ "What did I decide about release-codex earlier?": "You said no to the force push… open a pull request".
- **Explain-deep where recall happened to hit** (a0 r3, 11.1k window, 3 recalls, 7.3 s; r r3, r r5): the model found the opening in the lines `recall` returned, or answered from the window.
- The failure appears when the run is long and the probe needs the user's own first words: the model reaches for `recall`, and `recall` cannot return them (a0 r1: 7 calls, 17.7 s).

## 5. Proposed fixes, ranked by expected impact

All are labelled; none is judgment-in-code (no word list, regex or threshold decides what the user or a model meant).

1. **Information (harness): the simulated user declares which agenda item a line performs.** Replace "untagged line = next beat" in `agendaTurn` with a structured field on the user's turn (the item number it performed, or none for `[ANSWER]`/`[AGAIN]`/silence), and make a scripted silence a `TurnWait` that sends nothing to the orchestrator. Alternatively score `coherence_*` at run level with the judge quoting where the probe was asked (like `news_*`). Expected impact: removes the 13 artefact fails and the 9 vacuous passes; it also repairs `clarify_*` and `after_beat` cues, which share the beat tag. Files: `internal/eval/simuser.go` (`agendaTurn`, the tool schema and prompt), `internal/eval/simrun.go`, `internal/eval/segment.go`, `internal/eval/workday.go` (also drop the "voice connection was recycled" clause from the tier-1 criterion). Verify: unit tests in `internal/eval/segment_test.go` / `sim_test.go` for the mapping; at $0, rejudge a0 and r with coherence at run level (`sheepdog bench rejudge --from docs/bench/results/2026-10-07-chatty/a0`, copy the results first), expect the corrected counts above; then one fresh tier-1 workday ×5 on the unchanged product to check the artefact count falls to ≈ 0.
2. **Information (harness fidelity, possibly product): the typed line is recorded as the user's utterance.** In `brain.transcript` for a0 explain-deep r1 the kinds are `request: 18, reply, assistant_said, ...` and no `ceo_utterance`. With a voice the transcript gets a `KindCEOUtterance` per user turn (`internal/brain/brain.go:641`; `recall` searches it), but a typed tier-1 line exists only as a `KindRequest`, which `recall` skips (`internal/brain/tools.go:723`). Make the typed path ingest the user's verbatim words as `KindCEOUtterance` as the voice path does, so tier 1 measures what the user gets and a typed production session is no worse than a voiced one. Files: where the supervisor hands a typed utterance to `Brain.Turn` (`internal/supervisor`, `internal/brain/brain.go` ingest), checked with `internal/brain` tests. Do not simply unskip `KindRequest` in `recall`: it would return the supervisor's framing along with the words, and the voice path would then show each user line twice. Also soften `prompts/svp/RULES.md:13`: it says `recall` searches the full transcript; it skips tool, request, screen and promise lines. Expected impact: addresses rows 1 and 2 and the 10-05 explain-deep r5 and scale-ten r5 cells in tier 1. Verify: a unit test (a typed line is returned for a matching query); explain-deep ×5 per arm after fix 1 and compare first-thing probe pass and orchestrator turn time (a0 r1 took 17.7 s); `recall` is read-only, so no scale-ten safety run unless status wording changes.
3. **Information: let `recall` answer "from the start" and in any language.** (a) Tokenise on Unicode letters/digits instead of `a–z0–9` (`queryWords`), (b) an optional ordering parameter on the `recall` tool so the first N lines of the record can be asked for in order (`internal/brain/tools.go` tool declaration at ~line 101), (c) say in the result when it is truncated at 8 of N. Rationale: the first-thing probes need the oldest lines, and recall is newest-first capped at 8. Expected impact: removes the "nothing matches" and "only the latest" outcomes; row 1's 7 calls would be 1. Verify: tool unit tests; explain-deep and zh-evening ×5. Caveat: lexical overlap ranking is the existing retrieval, not a decision about meaning; if it feels like judgment, drop (a) and ship only (b) and (c), which are pure information.
4. **Information: tell the orchestrator where its record begins.** The prompt carries the clock ("## Now", `internal/brain/prompt.go:129`) but no call-start time and no marker that the record shown is complete since the start (or covers up to T after a compaction). Add one line to the state section. Rationale: row 1 hedged because the model could not know whether anything preceded the window. Expected impact: removes hedges of the kind "I can only see three minutes"; small, shares verification with 2. Verify: explain-deep ×5 and count answers containing a claim about missing history in the events.
5. **Do not change prompt wording for rows 3 and 4 yet.** Answering "what did you tell me earlier" with the present state is a model-habit problem (label if pursued: judgment in the model, prompt-only); `docs/eval/README.md` shows every prompt change so far lost on segment Understood. The judge was at 0.5–0.6 confidence on both; the gist is right. Revisit after fix 1 shows how many such cases are really failures.
6. **Judge consistency.** After fix 1 the "probe answered elsewhere" case disappears. Until then, treat `coherence_*` as unscored at run level, or rescore with a locator item. No rail applies: coherence failures are not safety failures and no rail would fix them.
7. **Tier 2 / resume.** The resume question has no segment-level evidence. Run `bench rejudge --segments` on the existing tier-2 campaigns with `runs.tar.gz` (10-05 workday t2: forced resumes at t+209–335 s in the report) to get coherence verdicts for the voice path at $0 API; do not start a new live run first.

## 6. Limits

- Classification is by reading the quoted evidence, the transcripts and the event logs; the judge's own confidence on the real fails is 0.5–0.9.
- No live run was made; all claims are from recorded results and source.
- "Correct" answers in the misaligned rows were checked against the scenario's `truth.coherence` text by reading, not re-judged.
- Counts are small (17 failing verdicts, 4 real); do not read a rate off them.
